# 08 · Year-End Adjustments: Depreciation & Provisions

Before closing a financial year, accountants post **adjusting entries** so the accounts reflect the true profit and position: depreciation, outstanding and prepaid expenses, accrued income, provisions and closing stock checks. This module walks through them in TallyPrime.

## 1. Depreciation

1. **Companies Act** (Schedule II) uses useful lives (e.g. furniture 10 years, computers 3 years, plant and machinery 15 years) with straight-line or written-down value; **income-tax depreciation** uses block-wise WDV rates (e.g. 10% furniture, 15% plant and machinery, 40% computers) with half-rate for assets used under 180 days in the year. The two often differ — book depreciation goes in the accounts; tax depreciation goes in the tax computation.
2. Example (book, WDV for illustration): Furniture opening WDV ₹2,40,000 at 10% → ₹24,000. Computer bought 1 October for ₹90,000; on income-tax rules, 40% × half year → ₹18,000.
3. TallyPrime entry (Journal): Dr Depreciation (Indirect Expenses) / Cr Furniture, Cr Computer.
4. Maintain a **fixed asset register** (TallyPrime's fixed asset features or a spreadsheet) with cost, additions, disposals and depreciation by asset.

## 2. Outstanding, prepaid and accrued items

1. **Outstanding expenses** (incurred, not paid): March electricity ₹14,000 → Dr Electricity / Cr Outstanding Expenses (Current Liabilities).
2. **Prepaid expenses** (paid in advance): insurance ₹36,000 paid for July–June → 3 months (April–June next year) prepaid ₹9,000 → Dr Prepaid Insurance (Current Assets) / Cr Insurance.
3. **Accrued income** (earned, not received): interest on FD ₹6,500 → Dr Accrued Interest / Cr Interest Received.
4. **Income received in advance**: Dr Income / Cr Advance Income (liability).
5. Use **reversing journals** (optional voucher class) at the start of the next year where appropriate.

## 3. Provisions

1. **Provision for doubtful debts**: estimate unrecoverable receivables (from the ageing report): e.g. 5% of debts over 90 days ₹3,20,000 → ₹16,000: Dr Bad Debts / Provision Expense / Cr Provision for Doubtful Debts.
2. **Bad debts written off**: Dr Bad Debts / Cr Customer (with GST considerations — output tax already paid isn't automatically recoverable).
3. **Provision for income tax** and **gratuity/leave** (based on estimates or actuarial valuation for larger companies).
4. **Provision for warranty** if products carry warranties.

## 4. Stock and other checks

1. Physical stock verification, write down damaged or obsolete stock to net realisable value.
2. Confirm balances with major customers and suppliers; reconcile all bank accounts.
3. Review GST: ITC reversals for unpaid suppliers (180 days), blocked credits, and match books to GSTR-3B for the year.
4. Review TDS: all deductions deposited and returns filed.
5. After adjustments, the year can be closed: TallyPrime carries balances forward automatically when you start entering in the new year (or split the company — Level 4).

## Cheat sheet

| Adjustment | Entry |
|---|---|
| Depreciation | Dr Depreciation / Cr Asset |
| Outstanding expense | Dr Expense / Cr Outstanding (liability) |
| Prepaid expense | Dr Prepaid (asset) / Cr Expense |
| Accrued income | Dr Accrued (asset) / Cr Income |
| Doubtful debts | Dr Provision expense / Cr Provision |
| Stock write-down | Value at lower of cost and NRV |

## How It Actually Works

Accounts are prepared on an **accrual basis**: income and expenses belong to the period in which they're earned or incurred, not when cash moves. Year-end adjustments move amounts into the right period and recognise the consumption of long-lived assets through depreciation, so profit reflects the year's real economic performance. Provisions apply prudence — recognising probable losses as soon as they can be estimated — which protects users of the accounts from overstated profits.

## Exercise

For Krishna Electricals at 31 March, post: depreciation on furniture and a computer bought in October (book and note tax depreciation), outstanding electricity and salary, prepaid insurance, accrued FD interest, a 5% provision on debts over 90 days and a stock write-down of ₹22,000. Compare the P&L before and after the adjustments.
