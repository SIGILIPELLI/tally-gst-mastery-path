# 06 · Accounting for Startups & MSMEs

Small businesses and startups have specific accounting needs: choosing a business structure, keeping founders' and business money separate, managing cash runway, using government MSME benefits and preparing for investors or lenders. This module covers the essentials. (General guidance — consult a CA for your situation.)

## 1. Business structure

1. **Sole proprietorship** — simplest; owner taxed personally; unlimited liability.
2. **Partnership / LLP** — shared ownership; LLP offers limited liability with lighter compliance than a company.
3. **Private limited company** — separate legal entity, limited liability, easier to raise equity; more compliance (ROC filings, audit).
4. The structure affects tax rates, compliance, fundraising and how TallyPrime capital accounts are set up (capital account vs share capital and reserves).

## 2. Clean books from day one

1. Separate bank account for the business; avoid personal expenses through the business (record them as **drawings** if they happen).
2. Founders' loans to the company: record as **unsecured loans** with documentation, not capital, unless shares are issued.
3. Record every invoice and expense with documents; claim GST ITC properly.
4. Choose accounting policies early: revenue recognition, depreciation, stock valuation.

## 3. MSME registration and benefits

1. **Udyam Registration** (udyamregistration.gov.in) — free, PAN/Aadhaar-based. Classification (revised 2025) by investment and turnover: micro, small and medium enterprises with higher limits than before — check current thresholds.
2. Benefits: priority sector lending, collateral-free loans under the CGTMSE scheme, government tender preferences, and protection against delayed payments.
3. **Delayed payment protection**: buyers must pay MSME suppliers within the agreed period (maximum **45 days**); under income-tax rules, payments to micro and small enterprises beyond the limit are disallowed as expenses until paid (section 43B(h) under the earlier Act, carried into the new law) — a reason buyers prioritise MSME invoices.
4. Record supplier MSME status in TallyPrime ledgers (MSME registration details) to track payment timelines and report outstanding dues (MSME-1 for companies).

## 4. Startup finance essentials

1. **Burn rate** = monthly cash outflow − inflow; **runway** = cash balance ÷ net burn. ₹60 lakh cash with ₹5 lakh monthly net burn = 12 months.
2. Monthly MIS with unit economics (CAC, gross margin, contribution per order) for founders and investors.
3. **DPIIT recognition** under Startup India offers benefits like tax holiday eligibility (section 80-IAC criteria under the earlier Act, with corresponding provisions), self-certification under labour laws and faster patent processing.
4. Equity rounds: record share capital and securities premium; maintain a cap table; ESOPs need careful accounting and tax treatment.
5. Investor due diligence expects clean, reconciled books, filed returns and statutory compliance — often the most expensive problem to fix late.

## How It Actually Works

Small businesses fail more often from running out of cash than from lack of profit. Clean, separate books make cash visible, support credit and funding applications, and keep tax deductions defensible. MSME payment rules shift bargaining power toward small suppliers by attaching tax consequences to late payment, which is why accurate MSME tagging of suppliers in TallyPrime now matters to buyers as well.
