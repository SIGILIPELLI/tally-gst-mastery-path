# 07 · Moving to an ERP: Tally, Zoho, SAP & Oracle

As businesses grow, they sometimes outgrow standalone accounting software and move to an **ERP (enterprise resource planning)** system that integrates finance with sales, procurement, manufacturing, HR and more. This module helps you judge when to move, compare options and run a migration.

## 1. Signs you may need more than TallyPrime alone

1. Many users across locations needing real-time access and complex approval workflows.
2. Complex manufacturing (MRP, shop-floor control), large warehouses, or multi-company consolidation with intercompany eliminations.
3. Heavy integrations: e-commerce, CRM, payment gateways, logistics, banks.
4. Investor or group reporting needs (Ind AS, global parent reporting).
5. Note: TallyPrime itself scales well for many SMEs with add-ons, TallyPrime Server, connectors and APIs — moving is a business decision, not a default.

## 2. Common options

| System | Typical fit |
|---|---|
| **TallyPrime (+ extensions)** | SMEs; strong Indian compliance; low cost; widely known by accountants |
| **Zoho Books / Zoho One** | Cloud-first SMEs and startups; good integrations; GST and e-invoicing support |
| **Busy, Marg** | Indian SMEs (trading, pharma distribution) |
| **Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central / Odoo** | Growing mid-size businesses wanting a broader ERP |
| **Oracle NetSuite** | Fast-growing companies, multi-entity, global operations |
| **SAP Business One** | Mid-size businesses |
| **SAP S/4HANA, Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP** | Large enterprises, complex operations, group reporting |

## 3. Evaluate properly

1. List requirements by priority: must-have (GST, e-invoicing, TDS, payroll integration, inventory), should-have, nice-to-have.
2. Total cost of ownership over 5 years: licences/subscriptions, implementation partner, customisation, training, hardware/cloud, support.
3. Ask for demos using **your** scenarios (a GST invoice with e-way bill, a job work cycle, month-end close).
4. Check partner experience in your industry and references.
5. Consider people: accountant availability and training for the chosen system.

## 4. Run the migration

1. Choose a cut-over date — usually the start of a financial year or quarter.
2. Clean data in TallyPrime first: remove duplicate ledgers, fix groupings, close old bills.
3. Migrate **masters** (chart of accounts, parties with GSTINs, items with HSN), **opening balances** (ledger, bill-wise, stock by godown/batch) and optionally historical transactions.
4. Run **parallel** for one period in both systems; reconcile trial balances, receivables, payables, stock and GST outputs.
5. Train users, document new processes, and keep TallyPrime data read-only for history and audits.

## Cheat sheet

| Step | Key action |
|---|---|
| Decide | Business need, not trend |
| Requirements | Must/should/nice-to-have |
| Cost | 5-year TCO |
| Demo | Your real scenarios |
| Migrate | Clean data, masters, opening balances |
| Validate | Parallel run + reconciliations |

## How It Actually Works

An ERP replaces separate systems and spreadsheets with one database shared by every department, so a sales order can automatically reserve stock, trigger procurement and post accounting entries. That integration delivers control and real-time visibility but brings complexity and cost; many ERP problems come from migrating messy data or redesigning processes poorly. Accountants who understand both TallyPrime and ERP concepts are valuable bridges during these transitions.
